Experiment 03 / Conditional survival

FVG age decay

If an FVG survives unfilled, does the label still add predictive value later?

Construction

Only zones that had survived to a given age were retained. The experiment then measured touch probability over the next interval for FVG survivors versus control survivors.

1m result

Scroll the table horizontally on smaller screens.

Condition Incremental FVG effect
Survived 1 bar → touch by bar 3 +3.09 pp
Survived 3 → touch by 5 −0.21 pp
Survived 5 → touch by 10 −0.08 pp
Survived 10 → touch by 20 −1.86 pp

Other timeframes

  • 5m: +2.39 pp after surviving 1 bar, then much smaller / mixed later.
  • 15m: +1.85 pp early, ~0 after 3 bars, negative at older ages.
  • 1H: +1.43 pp early, then negative after several bars.

Interpretation

The effect is concentrated near creation. An old unfilled FVG does not behave like a constant market “obligation.” The evidence is more consistent with a temporary information state that gets absorbed.

Conclusion

Freshness matters. Treating a newly formed gap and an old surviving gap as equivalent loses one of the clearest patterns in the data.

Exact implementation

Question asked conditionally

This is not another cumulative fill-rate table. For each matched sample the script first asks which zones are still untouched at an earlier horizon, and only then asks what fraction become touched by the later horizon.

Transitions

The frozen transitions are 1→3, 3→5, 5→10, and 10→20 native bars. The same transitions are evaluated separately on 1m, 5m, 15m, 1H and 4H.

Real FVG calculation

For a transition A→B, a real FVG enters the denominator only when its cumulative touch flag at A is zero. A success is a new touch by B. Thus the statistic is explicitly P(touch by B | survived through A).

Control calculation

The control side is deliberately calculated on individual matched controls that also survived A, rather than averaging controls first and conditioning later. This makes the survival condition apply to the actual synthetic zones.

Interpretive rule

If FVGs carried a persistent magnetic force, the conditional advantage should remain positive among survivors. Instead, the excess probability is concentrated in the earliest transition and generally collapses toward zero or negative values later.

Exact procedure

  1. Reuse the same real FVGs and matched control zones from the multi-timeframe attraction experiment.
  2. Compute cumulative touch outcomes by native-bar horizons 1, 3, 5, 10 and 20.
  3. For the 1→3 test, retain only FVGs that were still untouched after bar 1, then ask whether they became touched by bar 3.
  4. Apply the same survival condition to individual control zones and compute their conditional touch probability.
  5. Repeat the process for 3→5, 5→10 and 10→20.
  6. Report the difference between the FVG conditional touch rate and the control conditional touch rate at each age interval.

This removes already-filled gaps from later-age calculations. The question is not cumulative fill probability; it is whether an old surviving FVG still carries incremental information.

Reproduce it

for tf in 1 5 15 60 240; do
  python scripts/run_original.py multi-tf --tf $tf
done

The resulting decay_tf*.csv files contain survivor counts, FVG conditional touch probability, control conditional touch probability, and the difference.
