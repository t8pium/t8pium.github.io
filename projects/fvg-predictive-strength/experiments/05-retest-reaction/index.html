Experiment 05 / Support-resistance behavior

First-touch retest reaction

Once price returns to an FVG, does it react differently from a comparable ordinary zone?

Hypothesis

An FVG may be weak as a magnet yet still matter as a temporary support/resistance region. This experiment ignores whether price was “attracted” there and starts the test only after a retest occurs.

Construction

  1. Wait for price to reach the near edge of a real FVG or matched control zone.
  2. Define a rejection target one full zone width away from the zone.
  3. Race that rejection target against traversal through the full zone.
  4. Repeat with matched ordinary zones under similar market state.

A secondary measure tracked average movement away from the zone over the next three bars, normalized by ATR.

Movement away

Mean three-bar movement away from the zone was also positive relative to control on every tested timeframe: approximately +0.015 ATR on 1m, +0.014 on 5m, +0.017 on 15m, +0.025 on 1H, and +0.059 on 4H.

Conclusion

The reaction effect is small, but its sign is more consistent across timeframes than the magnet test. That suggests the more stable question may be how price behaves at an FVG, not whether an FVG permanently pulls price toward itself.

Exact implementation

Starting point

This experiment uses the same real FVGs and state-matched synthetic control zones as the multi-timeframe attraction test. It does not measure whether price is more likely to arrive. It conditions on a later retest and asks what happens once price reaches the zone.

First-touch search

For each zone, the Numba function first_touch_reaction scans from the bar after formation for at most 20 native bars. A bullish zone is considered touched when a low reaches its upper/near edge; a bearish zone when a high reaches its lower/near edge. The earliest such bar is the retest.

Barrier race

The race starts on the bar after the touch, preventing the touch bar itself from deciding the outcome. The rejection target is exactly one full gap width away from the near edge in the rejection direction. The competing failure barrier is the far edge / full fill. The race lasts at most 10 native bars.

  • +1: rejection target first.
  • -1: far edge first.
  • +2: both traded in the same native bar; order is unknowable from OHLC and is marked ambiguous.
  • 0: neither barrier resolved within the race horizon.

Control aggregation

For the binary race, only resolved non-ambiguous controls enter each parent FVG's control success rate. The real FVG is then paired with that parent-average control rate. A second metric records signed close movement three bars after the touch, measured from the near edge and normalized by ATR at FVG formation.

Why this is distinct from Experiment 07

This experiment uses native-timeframe OHLC and asks about a generic first-touch barrier reaction. The later CE/body experiment is a trade simulation: it requires an opposite-colored body close and resolves TP/SL on underlying future 1m bars.
