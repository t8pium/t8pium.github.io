Experiment 07 / Execution hypothesis

Candle-body acceptance around CE

Tests whether the opposing candle's body close depth inside the FVG—independent of wick length—changes the probability of rejection.

Setup

Penetration depth is defined from the near edge: 0% at the near edge, 50% at CE, and 100% at the far edge.

  • Bullish FVG → wait for a later bearish candle to enter the zone.
  • Bearish FVG → wait for a later bullish candle.
  • Classify only the candle-body close depth. Wick length is ignored.
  • Enter at the signal close; stop at the far FVG edge; target the near edge.

Exact 50%

For the cleaner ≥1H sample there were only 51 exact-midpoint closes. The unambiguous win rate was approximately 36.4% with about −0.27R/trade. Exact CE therefore failed as a universal standalone entry.

Before CE vs through CE

The most interesting split was not exact CE but body acceptance across it. On 1H, 45–50% closes produced about 57.6% WR / +0.089R, while 50–55% closes produced about 43.0% WR / −0.092R.

On 4H, the 45–50% band produced 49 trades, 33 wins, 16 losses, 67.35% WR and +0.286R/trade. A chronological later-30% slice had 15 trades and remained positive.

Multiple-testing warning

This is hypothesis-generating, not validated. The 4H 45–50% cell emerged after searching multiple timeframes, depth bands, exact-midpoint conditions, and first-touch variants. A small positive holdout does not erase that selection risk.

Conclusion

The data gives more support to a possible acceptance/rejection threshold around CE than to the idea that the exact 0.5 coordinate is special. Independent untouched replication is required.

Exact implementation

Timeframe construction

The execution study evaluates 1m, 2m, 3m, 5m, 10m, 15m, 30m, 1H, 2H, 4H, 6H, 8H, 12H and 1D. Intraday bars are grouped by CME trade date and minutes since the 18:00 ET session open. A higher-timeframe bar is discarded if its first and last one-minute rows do not belong to the same active symbol.

FVG eligibility

All three candles A/B/C must belong to the same active contract. The mechanical three-candle formula is unchanged, but this study additionally requires a minimum gap width of 0.25 index points = one MNQ tick.

Primary signal search

The primary mode is first qualifying opposite candle before body-close invalidation, not necessarily the first wick touch.

  • Bullish FVG: a later bearish candle must wick into the gap and close inside it.
  • Bearish FVG: a later bullish candle must wick into the gap and close inside it.
  • Wick length is otherwise irrelevant.
  • Bullish body-close invalidation occurs if a candle closes below the far/lower edge; bearish invalidation is a close above the far/upper edge.
  • Search stops at an active-contract change.

Depth formula

Depth is normalized so 0% is the near edge, 50% is CE and 100% is the far edge. Bullish depth is (upper - close) / width; bearish depth is (close - lower) / width. Only closes from 0% inclusive to 100% exclusive qualify.

Execution

Entry is the qualifying candle's close. TP is the near edge; SL is the far edge. The trigger higher-timeframe candle is mapped to its final underlying 1m row, and TP/SL scanning begins on the following one-minute bar. The scan ends at the active-contract boundary.

  • TP first = win.
  • SL first = loss.
  • TP and SL in the same 1m bar = ambiguous.
  • No resolution before roll/end = censored.

For the explicitly labeled conservative R, ambiguous trades are counted as −1R; unambiguous-only statistics exclude them. MFE, MAE and minutes-to-exit are also recorded.

Reward/risk geometry

Because entry moves deeper into the gap, reward and risk change with depth. At normalized depth x, theoretical breakeven win rate is 1 − x. The summary therefore compares observed win rate with the average per-trade geometric breakeven rate rather than always assuming 50%.

Predefined and exploratory slices

The script reports 10%-wide depth buckets plus exact 50%, 49–51%, 47.5–52.5%, 45–55% and 40–60%. It also reruns the 45–55% test under a stricter first raw wick touch must qualify mode. The later 4H 45–50% result was sliced after inspecting the depth/timeframe table and is therefore explicitly post-hoc/hypothesis-generating.

Exact signal search

  1. Build 1m through daily bars from the active one-minute series. Intraday bars are anchored to the 18:00 ET CME session; daily bars use the stored CME trade date.
  2. Require all three FVG candles to belong to the same listed contract and require at least one MNQ tick of gap width.
  3. After formation, scan forward while the same active listed contract remains in force.
  4. For a bullish FVG, the qualifying candle must be bearish; for a bearish FVG, it must be bullish.
  5. The wick must touch the gap, but wick length is otherwise ignored.
  6. Compute body-close depth: 0% at the near edge, 50% at CE, 100% at the far edge.
  7. The trigger close must remain inside [0%,100%). Search terminates if an earlier candle body closes beyond the far edge.
  8. Save both the first qualifying opposite candle and a stricter sensitivity where the very first raw wick touch must itself qualify.

Exact execution logic

  1. Enter at the qualifying timeframe candle's close.
  2. Target the near edge of the FVG; stop at the far edge.
  3. Map the trigger candle to its ending one-minute base bar.
  4. From the next one-minute bar forward, resolve target/stop until one is hit or the active listed-contract segment ends.
  5. If target and stop trade inside the same one-minute bar, assign outcome code +2 = ambiguous.
  6. Conservative expectancy counts ambiguity as -1R; censored trades remain NaN.
  7. Measure MFE, MAE, exit time, realized R and theoretical breakeven win rate implied by the actual entry depth.
  8. Aggregate 10%-wide depth buckets plus exact 50%, 49–51%, 47.5–52.5%, 45–55% and 40–60% slices.
  9. Repeat by timeframe, year, direction, first-touch sensitivity and chronological 70/30 split.
Why low-timeframe ambiguity is high: tiny FVGs can place both TP and SL inside the same one-minute OHLC bar. The code refuses to invent which traded first.
