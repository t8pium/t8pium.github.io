Experiment 09 / Stability through time

Out-of-sample robustness

Chronological holdouts test whether the apparent effect persists after the period used to discover it.

Design

Matched observations were split chronologically: the first 70% for research and the last 30% as a later-period robustness check. The time series was never randomly shuffled.

Longer-horizon 1m holdout

In a separate 60-minute study, the first 70% showed an FVG-control difference of about +0.79 pp. In the later 30%, the difference shrank to only +0.08 pp.

This is one of the strongest arguments against treating the full-sample p-value as proof of a durable trading edge.

Year stability

The 60-minute incremental effect weakened across the sample:

  • 2020: +1.55 pp
  • 2021: +0.80 pp
  • 2022: +0.50 pp
  • 2023: +0.85 pp
  • 2024: +0.32 pp
  • 2025: −0.30 pp
  • 2026 partial sample: −0.05 pp

What survived best?

  • More robust: 1m/5m first-few-bar attraction, retest reaction, distance dominance, age decay.
  • Less robust: 1H+ attraction, persistent 60m+ magnetism, year-stable CE behavior, exact-50 execution.

Conclusion

The evidence is consistent with a real but non-stationary, decaying effect. The short-horizon lower-timeframe component survives best; persistent higher-timeframe magnetism does not.

Exact implementation

Multi-timeframe holdout

For each timeframe's matched attraction sample, the five-native-bar real/control pairs are sorted by formation timestamp. The earliest 70% form the research/train segment and the latest 30% the test segment. The split is chronological; rows are never shuffled.

Deep 1m holdout

The deep 1m matched sample applies its own 70th-percentile timestamp cutoff to the 60-minute touch outcome. In the published run the cutoff was 2024-07-09 01:56:36+00:00. The same script also reports the 60-minute real/control difference separately by calendar year.

CE/body split

The CE program sorts qualifying resolved 45–55% trades by trigger_ts and applies a 70/30 chronological split globally and by timeframe. Because the broad 45–55% rule was specified before examining narrower cells, this is useful robustness evidence for that broad rule.

Why the 4H 45–50% “test30” is not a clean validation

The narrower 4H 45–50% band was discovered after inspecting many timeframe/depth combinations. Splitting that already-selected subgroup 70/30 does not turn the last 30% into a truly untouched confirmatory sample. The page therefore labels it hypothesis-generating despite its positive later slice.

What a stronger next validation requires

Freeze the exact 4H 45–50% rule without changing any parameter and test it on data or markets not used to select the cell—for example later MNQ history, NQ under the same rules, or other US index futures. That is the point at which the result can begin moving from discovery to confirmation.

Exact validation design

  1. Never randomly shuffle the time series into train and test rows.
  2. In the multi-timeframe study, sort matched FVG parent events chronologically and split them at 70% of the matched-event count.
  3. Recompute the five-native-bar FVG touch rate, parent-averaged control touch rate and paired difference separately in the first 70% and final 30%.
  4. In the detailed one-minute study, use the 70th percentile of event timestamps as the chronological cutoff.
  5. Recompute the 60-minute matched attraction difference independently before and after that timestamp.
  6. Separately stratify the detailed matched 60-minute experiment by calendar year to reveal non-stationarity that a single full-sample statistic can hide.
  7. For the CE/body-close study, sort qualifying trades by trigger timestamp and repeat 70/30 splits for the 45–55% band globally and by timeframe where sample size permits.

The holdout is a robustness check, not a claim that the last 30% was never examined after the fact. Once these results were viewed, they became part of the research history; future confirmation requires newer or independent data.

Reproduce it

# Multi-timeframe five-bar holdouts
