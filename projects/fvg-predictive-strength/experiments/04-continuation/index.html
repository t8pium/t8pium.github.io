Experiment 04 / Directional prediction

Formation continuation

Does the displacement that creates an FVG predict continued movement in the same direction?

Control design

FVG-forming three-bar moves were matched against non-FVG moves with similar direction, total displacement, middle-candle body size, volatility, trend, session, and time bucket. Forward returns were normalized by ATR and signed in the direction of formation.

Robustness

The signs continued to flip when the horizon changed. For example, the 1m 10-bar difference was positive while the 1H 10-bar difference was negative. No monotonic timeframe or horizon relationship emerged.

Conclusion

The data does not support a general rule that creating a bullish FVG predicts bullish continuation or vice versa. Attraction and retest reaction are more defensible FVG properties than formation-direction continuation.

Exact implementation

Why a second control design?

The matched-zone magnet experiment asks whether the location of an FVG is special. Formation continuation asks whether the move that creates it is special. Controls therefore match the displacement event rather than transplanting a zone.

FVG event features

The script calculates the signed direction from the close two bars ago to the formation close, absolute three-bar movement divided by ATR, and the middle candle's absolute body divided by ATR. Movement is bucketed at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3 ATR; middle-body size at 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5 ATR.

Matched non-FVG event

A candidate must be a valid non-FVG bar with the same session, volatility regime, trend regime, time bucket, signed displacement direction, three-bar movement bucket, and middle-body bucket. Up to 8,000 FVG events are sampled per timeframe, and one matched non-FVG event is randomly drawn for each.

Outcome

Forward return at 1, 3, 5 and 10 native bars is multiplied by the original FVG direction and divided by ATR at the event. The matched event is scored using the same directional sign. Positive values therefore mean continuation in the direction that created the FVG.

Exact matching logic

  1. Measure the three-bar move magnitude ending on the FVG formation bar, normalized by ATR.
  2. Measure the middle candle's absolute body size, also normalized by ATR.
  3. Classify the move direction from candle A close to candle C close.
  4. Construct non-FVG candidate buckets requiring the same session, volatility regime, trend regime, time-of-day bucket, direction, move-size bin and middle-body-size bin.
  5. Randomly sample up to 8,000 FVG events per timeframe and select one matched non-FVG move from the exact same bucket.
  6. Measure forward return after 1, 3, 5 and 10 native bars, sign it in the original formation direction and divide by ATR.
  7. Compare mean directional return and positive-return frequency between FVG-forming moves and matched non-FVG displacements.

This experiment isolates formation continuation from later retest behavior. A negative difference means a similar non-FVG displacement continued more strongly.
