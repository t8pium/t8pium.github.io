Experiment 01 / Baseline behavior

Raw fill rates

The first experiment measures what FVGs do without pretending that a high fill percentage automatically proves anything special.

Hypothesis

If FVGs are genuinely special magnets, their raw revisit rate should be high—but a valid conclusion also requires an ordinary-zone baseline.

Procedure

For the mechanically detected one-minute FVG population, future paths were checked for initial touch, midpoint mitigation, full fill, multiple fixed horizons, and eventual touch before the dataset ended. Right-censored events were not silently discarded.

Baseline problem

At roughly three trading days the matched control-zone touch rate was about 98.97%, slightly above the FVG rate of about 98.95%. By one trading day both were roughly 98.2%.

Result: “Almost every FVG eventually fills” is descriptively true and inferentially weak. MNQ revisits nearby prices extremely often.

Conclusion

Raw fill rate is not a useful proof of FVG-specific attraction. The rest of the study therefore focuses on incremental effects versus controls, not the headline percentage.

Exact implementation

Population

This experiment uses the canonical deep one-minute program. A 1m FVG is eligible only after the 14-bar ATR, 60-bar ATR-normalized trend score, and long volatility baseline are available, and it is excluded inside the ±1-day roll block. Gap width must be positive and its near edge cannot already be beyond price in the wrong direction at formation.

What counts as a touch?

For a bullish FVG, a touch occurs when a future low reaches the upper/near edge; 50% mitigation occurs when a future low reaches the midpoint; full fill occurs when a future low reaches the lower/far edge. Bearish rules are symmetric using future highs.

Horizons

The fixed horizons are 5, 15, 30, 60, 120, 240, 1,380, and 4,140 one-minute trading bars. The code computes future extrema with rolling minima/maxima on reversed arrays, avoiding a Python path scan for every event. Eventual touch and eventual full fill are calculated with suffix minima/maxima through the end of the available dataset.

Why the raw percentage is not the conclusion

The raw population is used for descriptive fill rates. The inferential comparison uses a seeded sample of up to 40,000 real FVGs and 5 controls per FVG. A control has the same direction and its width/distance are transplanted in ATR units at a non-FVG timestamp matched on session, New York hour, volatility regime, and trend regime. RNG seed: 42.

Outputs

The script writes main_results.csv, the full FVG event table, sensitivity tables, directional tables, train/test results, stratified results and charts. The reference CSV committed with the project is the exact published output.

python scripts/run_original.py detailed-1m

Exact procedure

  1. Build 14-period ATR on the active one-minute series and exclude roll-neighborhood bars.
  2. Detect bullish gaps when low[t] > high[t-2] and bearish gaps when high[t] < low[t-2].
  3. Reject zero/negative-width gaps and events whose measured starting distance is negative.
  4. For every eligible event, precompute future rolling minima/maxima for 5, 15, 30, 60, 120, 240, 1,380 and 4,140 trading minutes.
  5. For each horizon, test three thresholds separately: near-edge touch, midpoint mitigation, and far-edge/full fill.
  6. Separately compute future suffix minima/maxima to determine eventual touch and eventual full fill anywhere later in the available sample.
  7. Do not count the FVG formation bar itself as a future touch.

This experiment is intentionally descriptive. Its purpose is to establish the raw behavior that later control experiments must falsify.

Reproduce it

git clone https://github.com/t8pium/fvg-predictive-strength.git
cd fvg-predictive-strength
python scripts/run_original.py detailed-1m

The raw fill outputs are produced by the original detailed one-minute script, alongside the matched-control and regime outputs.
