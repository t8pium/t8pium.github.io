Experiment 02 / Magnet test

Matched-zone attraction

Real FVGs are compared with ordinary price zones that have similar geometry and market context.

Hypothesis

H0: after controlling for distance, width, volatility, trend and session, price is no more likely to reach an FVG than an ordinary zone.

H1: the FVG label increases the probability of touch beyond those controls.

Directional check

Average movement toward the target was positive relative to controls for the first ~1–10 bars, then disappeared and reversed at longer horizons. That distinguishes a short-lived directional attraction from a level that merely gets revisited eventually.

Conclusion

Supported: a small short-horizon lower-timeframe attraction bias. Not supported: persistent magnetism or stronger attraction simply because the FVG is higher timeframe.

Exact implementation

Two independent implementations

The attraction result was tested twice. The deep 1m program samples up to 40,000 FVGs with 5 controls each. The multi-timeframe program tests 1m, 5m, 15m, 1H and 4H separately, sampling up to 12,000 FVGs per timeframe with 3 controls each.

Matching variables

For each real FVG, the control keeps the same bullish/bearish direction, ATR-normalized gap width, and ATR-normalized starting distance from price. Multi-timeframe controls are selected from non-FVG bars with the exact same session, volatility bucket, trend bucket, and time bucket. Time buckets are one hour through 1H and four hours above 1H. The deep 1m version matches exact New York hour. Controls are sampled with replacement only when a matching pool is too small.

Touch calculation

For each native horizon 1/3/5/10/20 bars, future minimum and maximum arrays are precomputed. A bullish zone is touched if the future minimum reaches the near edge; a bearish zone is touched if the future maximum reaches the near edge. The reported control probability for inference is the parent-average of that FVG's controls, so a real event remains the unit of comparison.

Uncertainty

The multi-timeframe program clusters differences by New York calendar day and bootstraps whole days 300 times. The deep 1m program uses a paired day-cluster bootstrap with a separate seed 7. This avoids pretending thousands of FVGs from the same trading day are independent.

Directional attraction

The deep 1m program also measures signed movement toward the zone after 1, 3, 5, 10, 15, 30 and 60 bars. For a bullish FVG, falling price toward the gap is positive; for a bearish FVG, rising price toward the gap is positive. Values are normalized by ATR at formation.
